> **How to read "fiber", "func" and "canonicalized LLC" in this note (`[paper]`).** Every canonicalization used here (§2) is a loss-preserving invertible map $g$ of parameter space: coordinatewise rescalings $(\gamma_i,\beta_i,W_{:,i})\mapsto(s_i\gamma_i,s_i\beta_i,s_i^{-1}W_{:,i})$ with $s_i>0$, per-head $GL(d_h)$ actions on full-rank $Q$–$K$ and $V$–$O$ factors, positive ReLU neuron rescalings, sign flips and permutations, and translations along exactly flat directions (the $\mathbf{1}_d$ kernel and bias absorption). For such $g$, $K\circ g=K$, so the true LLC satisfies $\lambda(w)=\lambda(g\cdot w)$ (Lau et al., 2308.12108, §3 and App. C: invariance under local diffeomorphisms). In this note, therefore:
> - $\hat\lambda_{\text{raw}}$ and $\hat\lambda_{\text{bal}}$ are two SGLD estimates of the **same** true LLC, taken at two parameters that implement the same function. $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{fiber}}=\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{total}}-\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{func}}$ measures how much the change in the **estimator** comes from its non-invariance under these symmetries (isotropic localization $\frac{\gamma}{2}\|w-w^*\|^2$, finite $n\beta$, finite step size and chain length). It is **not** a change in the true LLC. This differs from our earlier LoRA setting, where balancing could move between strata of the fiber (rank changes), so the true $\lambda$ could genuinely differ.
> - "Fiber sliding" names a real fact about **training**: from 25k to 80k the trajectory moves a long way along an exact rescaling orbit (pre-attention LN $\gamma$ shrinks while $W_{QKV}$ grows). What is an artifact is that the SGLD **estimate** tracks this coordinate.
> - "Balanced / canonicalized LLC" means **the SGLD estimate at the norm-balanced representative**. It is itself a finite-$\gamma$ estimate and is gauge-dependent: at 80k (seed 0, §5.1) three gauges give 158.8 (`LN0_attn` only), 160.7 (`FULL`) and 163.2 (unit-$\gamma$). It is not the true LLC either. What it shows is that a symmetry-fixed estimate has no LR3–LR4 drop.
> - Prior work: Lau et al. (2308.12108) note that the SGLD estimator equals the LLC only conjecturally, under conditions on $\gamma$ (App. D). Their rescaling-invariance experiment (App. O, two-layer ReLU network) used a preconditioner matched to the rescaling, because with the original algorithm the anisotropy forced "prohibitively small step sizes". So the estimator's non-invariance under rescaling is implicit in prior work. What we have not found in prior work is (i) a training trajectory drifting along such an orbit and (ii) that drift producing a reported developmental LLC drop.

## Executive Summary

We apply our exact full-model iso-functional fiber decomposition (`experiments/transformer_fiber_surgery.py`, Part 3) to Hoogland, Wang, Farrugia-Roberts, Carroll, Wei & Murfet (2024, TMLR, arXiv:2402.02364), *"Loss Landscape Degeneracy and Stagewise Development in Transformers"* — specifically their **51,717-parameter in-context linear regression Transformer (`L2H4Minf`)**, whose non-monotonic SGLD Local Learning Coefficient (LLC) curve exhibits five plateaus and a striking late-stage LLC drop (`LR1`–`LR5`) interpreted as developmental stages.

Their regression checkpoints are not public, so we **retrained** 3 seeds (0, 1, 2) ourselves. Architecture, task distribution and optimizer (`Adam, wd=0`, `OneCycleLR`) follow their paper (App. F.2), but the schedule is **5× shorter** ($T=100$k vs. their 500k). The SGLD settings $\epsilon = 3\times 10^{-4}, \gamma = 13.3, n\beta = 66.7$ follow their Table 2. The multi-seed grid uses a cheaper chain than theirs: 4 chains (vs. 10), 1,000 burn-in + 2,500 draws (vs. 1,000 + 4,000), minibatch $m=256$ (vs. 1024), and a fixed 32,768-sample pool (vs. $2^{20}$). Their exact chain length and $m$ are used only in the seed-0 checks (§5–6). Exact float64 canonicalization ($\max|\Delta y| \le 1.47\times 10^{-12}$ across all 48 multi-seed checkpoints) gives the following **dichotomy between early development and the late-stage drop in the SGLD estimate (`[empirical]`):**

1. **The early-stage rises (`LR1` zero prediction, `LR2` in-context learning acquisition) are not gauge artifacts: the balanced estimate rises as much as the raw one (`[empirical]`):**
   - `LR1 (0 → 1k)`: $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{total}} = +28.09 \pm 5.35$ decomposes into $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{func}} = +28.11 \pm 5.32$ and $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{fiber}} = -0.02 \pm 0.05$ (**fiber share $-0.1\% \pm 0.2\%$**).
   - `LR2 (1k → 25k)`: $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{total}} = +127.10 \pm 0.98$ decomposes into $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{func}} = +135.54 \pm 1.35$ and $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{fiber}} = -8.44 \pm 0.40$ (**fiber share $-6.6\% \pm 0.3\%$**; the balanced estimate rises slightly *more* than the raw one).
2. **The `LR3–LR4` drop in the SGLD estimate (`25k → 80k`) equals the growth of the raw-minus-balanced gap ($101.2\% \pm 1.1\%$ of the drop). Raw and balanced parameters implement the same function at each checkpoint, so that gap is the estimator's non-invariance along exact symmetry orbits. The SGLD estimate at the balanced representative shows no net drop (`[empirical]`; interpretation per the box above):**
   - Across all 3 seeds, the raw SGLD estimate falls by $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{-46.76 \pm 4.52}$ (`160.29 → 113.53`). This is comparable to the 5-seed mean in their released `llcs.pt` (`169.9` at ~40k → `123.4` at ~320k, $-46.6$). Their seed 1, the run in their Fig. 1b table (LR3 $-12.3$, LR4 $-44.1$), drops $-56.5$, and the 5 seeds range from $-39$ to $-57$. Our drop happens on a 5× compressed schedule and from a ~10-point lower peak.
   - On the **exact same functions** (canonicalized in float64 to $\max|\Delta y| \le 1.1\times 10^{-12}$ on 64 check sequences, before the float32 cast used for SGLD; same true LLC, see box), the balanced estimate shows no net drop: $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{func}} = \mathbf{+0.64 \pm 0.55}$ (`159.45 → 160.09`). It is not perfectly flat: it dips by `1.5–2.5` points at 50k in all three seeds (`157.36` mean) before recovering, and seed 2 ends at $-0.07$, leaving $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{fiber}} = \mathbf{-47.40 \pm 4.86}$ (**fiber share $101.2\% \pm 1.1\%$**, individually `103.4%`, `100.5%`, `99.8%` across seeds `0, 1, 2`).
3. **Per-channel ablation (seed 0) isolates one symmetry direction: pre-attention `LN_0 ↔ QKV` coordinatewise rescaling (`[empirical]` + `[paper]`):**
   - At step `80k` (seed 0, exact $T_{\mathrm{SGLD}} = 5{,}000$), `RAW` gives `109.06 ± 1.15` vs. `FULL` `160.72 ± 0.38` (`+51.65`).
   - Balancing **only** the two pre-attention LayerNorms (`blk0_ln0`, `blk1_ln0`) against `W_QKV` via the exact symmetry $(\gamma_{0,i}, \beta_{0,i}) \mapsto s_i(\gamma_{0,i}, \beta_{0,i})$, $W_{QKV,:,i} \mapsto s_i^{-1} W_{QKV,:,i}$ ($\max|\Delta y| = 6.22\times 10^{-15}$) lifts the estimate from `109.06` to **`158.82 ± 0.31`** (**`+49.76` points, `96.3%` of the 80k `FULL`–`RAW` gap**), while reducing split-half drift from `+2.2` to `+0.2`. Over 25k → 80k, the seed-0 ablation estimates change by `-49.98` (`RAW`), `-2.83` (`LN0_attn` only) and `+1.79` (`FULL`). Balancing `LN_0 ↔ QKV` alone therefore removes `94.3%` of the raw decline.
   - `LN1_mlp` (`Pre-MLP LN_1 ↔ fc1`) accounts for `+10.94` individually; `LN_mean_proj` (`+0.00`), `QK` (`-0.39`), `VO` (`+0.92`), `MLP` (`-0.22`), and `LNf_unembed` (`-0.30`) are all $< 1$ point.
4. **Bearing on Hoogland et al.'s "LayerNorm collapse" conjecture (their §7.3: collapse "possibly contributing to the LLC decrease") (`[paper]` + `[empirical]`):**
   - The LN collapse they describe does happen in our runs. The final unembedding LayerNorm (`lnf`) collapses in effective weight (`77%` of $|\gamma_{f,i}| < 0.1$, `82%` of effective products $|\gamma_{f,i} W_{U,0,i}| < 0.05$). The 3-seed-mean OOD `g=5` normalized MSE rises from `2.927` at 50k to `2.986` at 80k, but only seeds 1 and 2 show this; seed 0 keeps improving (`2.817 → 2.803`). However, `lnf` involves only 64 scalar weights, and balancing it does **not** remove the $-47$-point drop in the estimate.
   - By contrast, the earlier pre-attention LayerNorms (`blk0_ln0`, `blk1_ln0`) shrink (`mean|γ|: 0.797 → 0.246` and `0.913 → 0.422`) **in anti-correlation with a $3.2\text{--}3.6\times$ blowup of downstream `W_QKV` (`13.27 → 42.18` and `14.19 → 51.14`)**, keeping `93%–100%` of effective products live (`mean_eff: 1.337 → 1.398` and `1.634 → 2.747`). Each scalar $\gamma_{0,i}$ multiplies $3 d_{\text{embed}} = 192$ downstream weights (`24,576` parameters in total, `47.5%` of the model). In a local quadratic (Laplace) heuristic, shrinking $\gamma_i$ by $c_i < 1$ scales the curvature of those $192$ directions by $c_i^2$. That pushes them below the isotropic localization floor $\gamma / (n\beta)$ and lowers $\hat\lambda_{\text{SGLD}}$ without changing the implemented function or the true LLC. (`[paper]` heuristic; Hessian spectra were not measured.)
5. **Supporting controls: localization strength, full-batch gradients, and weight decay (`[empirical]`; seed 0 for the SGLD controls):**
   - **Localization strength ($T_{\mathrm{SGLD}} = 5{,}000$):** With localization weakened $4\times$ to $\gamma = 3.3$, the raw–balanced gap at 80k shrinks to `-2.98` (`RAW 161.13 ± 0.84` vs. `FULL 164.11 ± 0.59`). The raw 25k → 80k drop shrinks from about $-49$ to $-6.9$ (`168.02 → 161.13`), but it is not gone, and at 25k the gap reverses sign (`+7.52`). At $\gamma = 3.3$ the chains also travel $\|w-w^*\|\approx 125$ from $w^*$, comparable to $\|w^*\|\approx 107$, so the estimate is no longer local. Weaker $\gamma$ should not be read as "closer to the true LLC". Strengthening localization to $\gamma = 40.0$ widens the gap to **`-94.78` points** (`RAW 64.10 ± 0.31` vs. `FULL 158.88 ± 0.52`). The raw estimate swings by 97 points across $\gamma$; the balanced one moves by 5.
   - **Minibatch and full-batch-gradient controls (step 80k, $\gamma = 13.3$, $T_{\mathrm{SGLD}} = 5{,}000$):** Their $m = 1024$ reproduces $m = 256$ to $0.2$ points (`RAW 109.96 ± 1.61` vs. `FULL 160.90 ± 0.25`, gap `-50.94`). **Full-batch-gradient SGLD** on a fixed 1,024-sample set removes minibatch gradient noise but keeps the Langevin noise $\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\xi$. It preserves the gap (`RAW 106.40 ± 1.68` vs. `FULL 152.88 ± 0.20`, gap `-46.48`, split-half drift `-0.2`; its LLC is relative to that 1,024-sample loss). This is consistent with the drop being a property of the localized posterior rather than of minibatch noise or obvious non-stationarity.
   - **Weight-decay counterfactual (`AdamW, wd = 0.08`, 3 seeds; a different trajectory and different functions, so supporting rather than decisive):** Bounding weight norms (`||w||: 24.9 → 27.3` vs. `108.2` under `wd=0`) prevents the `LN_0 ↔ QKV` blowup (`||W_QKV,0|| = 14.0` vs. `42.2`). It also leaves **no drop in the estimate over 25k → 80k** ($\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{total}} = +2.48 \pm 1.61$, $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{func}} = +2.37 \pm 1.81$, $\Delta\hat\lambda_{\text{fiber}} = +0.11 \pm 0.23$). This holds even though `AdamW` collapses `lnf` further (`94% < 0.1`, `94%` effective `< 0.05`) and OOD `g=5` MSE rises (`3.259` at 10k → `3.543` at 80k).

## 2. Full-Model Exact Iso-Functional Canonicalization & Float64 Verification (`[paper]` + `[empirical]`)

We canonicalize the following exact continuous symmetries of `InContextRegressionTransformer` in `float64`, using 15 alternating sweeps of the coupled channels to reduce the parameter norm along the exact iso-functional fiber. This is **not** the complete symmetry group and **not** a global norm minimizer. For example, the 4 unused unembedding rows and biases (the regression readout uses only output channel 0) are left untouched, although zeroing them would lower the norm without changing predictions. The `LNf_unembed` move preserves the regression output (readout row 0) but not the unused `DTransformer` output channels. `max|Δy|` is measured on 64 sequences in float64, before the canonical weights are cast back to float32 for SGLD:

1. **Pre-LN $\mathbf{1}_d$ kernel projection (`LN_mean_proj`, `[paper]`):** Because every residual-stream reader (`blk0.ln0`, `blk0.ln1`, `blk1.ln0`, `blk1.ln1`, `lnf`) passes through LayerNorm first, projecting out the constant vector $\mathbf{1}_{64}$ from every column of `token_embedding`, `postn_embedding`, `attention.output`, `fc2.weight`, and `fc2.bias` leaves all outputs identically invariant.
2. **Per-head $GL(d_h)$ $Q$-$K$ balancing (`QK`, `[paper]`):** For each layer and head $h \in \{0,\dots,3\}$, replace $(W_Q^{(h)}, W_K^{(h)})$ with the exact rank-$d_h$ balanced SVD $( \Sigma_{:d_h}^{1/2} U_{:,:d_h}^\top,\; \Sigma_{:d_h}^{1/2} V_{:,:d_h}^\top )$ of $(W_Q^{(h)})^\top W_K^{(h)}$. Minimizes $\|W_Q^{(h)}\|_F^2 + \|W_K^{(h)}\|_F^2$. This is a $GL(d_h)$ action only when $W_Q^{(h)}, W_K^{(h)}$ have full rank $d_h$; for rank-deficient heads it would change stratum. In the audit spot check (below), every head has full rank.
3. **Per-head $GL(d_h)$ $V$-$O$ balancing (`VO`, `[paper]`):** Replace $(W_O^{(h)}, W_V^{(h)})$ with the exact rank-$d_h$ balanced SVD of $W_O^{(h)} W_V^{(h)}$.
4. **ReLU MLP positive neuronwise rescaling & sorting (`MLP`, `[paper]`):** Because `DTransformer` uses ReLU, scaling $(w_{1,j}, b_{1,j}) \mapsto s_j (w_{1,j}, b_{1,j})$ and $w_{2,:,j} \mapsto s_j^{-1} w_{2,:,j}$ with $s_j = (\|w_{2,:,j}\|_2 / \sqrt{\|w_{1,j}\|_2^2 + b_{1,j}^2})^{1/2}$ minimizes squared norm while preserving outputs identically; neurons are then canonically sorted by descending effective product.
5. **LayerNorm affine $(\gamma, \beta) \leftrightarrow W_{\text{next}}$ coordinatewise balancing (`LNf_unembed`, `LN1_mlp`, `LN0_attn`, `[paper]`):**
   - For biased downstream layers (`lnf → unembedding[0]` and `ln1 → fc1`): absorb $\beta$ into downstream bias ($b' = b + W\beta$, $\beta' = 0$) and balance $|\gamma_i'| = \|W'_{:,i}\|_2 = \sqrt{|\gamma_i|\|W_{:,i}\|_2}$ (setting both to $0$ if the product is exactly $0$; that branch is **not** a group action and could change stratum, but it only fires on exact zeros).
   - For biasless `ln0 → QKV`: simultaneous coordinatewise rescaling $(\gamma_{0,i}, \beta_{0,i}) \mapsto s_i(\gamma_{0,i}, \beta_{0,i})$ and $W_{QKV,:,i} \mapsto s_i^{-1} W_{QKV,:,i}$ with $s_i = (\|W_{QKV,:,i}\|_2 / \sqrt{\gamma_{0,i}^2 + \beta_{0,i}^2})^{1/2}$ is **exact even without an attention bias**.
   - *Non-exact move excluded:* Residual-stream global rescaling is **not** exact because PyTorch `LayerNorm` has $\varepsilon = 10^{-5} > 0$, so it is intentionally excluded from our exact canonicalizer.

**Exactness verification across every single move and checkpoint (`[empirical]`):**
- Individual moves at step `80k` (seed 0, float64 on 64 held-out sequences): `LN_mean_proj` `7.11e-15`, `QK` `3.13e-14`, `VO` `3.91e-14`, `MLP` `4.33e-15`, `LNf_unembed` `1.78e-15`, `LN1_mlp` `8.83e-15`, `LN0_attn` `6.22e-15`, `LN_all_unit_gamma` `1.15e-14`, `FULL` `2.48e-13`.
- **Audit spot check (independent retrain of Adam seed 0 from the same code; checkpoints at 25k/80k; 512 fresh sequences in float64):** every channel preserves outputs to $\le 7.6\times10^{-13}$ (`FULL`). `LN0_attn` is a pure coordinatewise rescaling: $\gamma W$ and $\beta W$ products are preserved to $\le 4\times10^{-16}$, with all $s_i>0$ ($s_i\in[2.6, 40.8]$ in block 0 at 80k). No group action passes through zero. The smallest singular values of the per-head $Q,K,V,O$ factors are $\ge 0.26$, the smallest MLP fan-in/fan-out norms are $\ge 0.66$, and the only exact zeros `FULL` creates are the 192 absorbed LN biases (a translation along a flat direction). One caveat: at 80k some `lnf` coordinates have $|\gamma_{f,i}|\sim10^{-11}$ and $|\gamma_{f,i}w_{0,i}|\sim10^{-23}$, numerically on the zero-product locus in both gauges. `FULL` is converged: 15 and 60 sweeps give the same norm to $10^{-4}$. The retrain matches the JSON's seed-0 LN statistics to 3 digits at 25k. By 80k, MPS nondeterminism has moved it slightly ($\|w\|$ `107.6` vs. `106.6`).

### 5.3 Mathematical Mechanism: The $1$-vs-$d_{\text{out}}$ Finite-$\gamma$ Curvature Distortion (`[paper]`)
For a coordinate $i$ entering a linear layer via $\gamma_i W_{:, i} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}}$, rescaling $(\gamma_i, W_{:, i}) \mapsto (c_i \gamma_i, c_i^{-1} W_{:, i})$ with $c_i \ll 1$ leaves the function identical, yet transforms the local curvature asymmetrically. The statements below are about **diagonal** (coordinate-direction) curvatures; the full Hessian transforms by congruence $DHD$, so its eigenvalues do not simply rescale, and we did not compute them:
- The **single** direction $\delta \gamma_i$ sees its curvature multiplied by $c_i^{-2} \gg 1$ (already saturated at $\frac{1}{2} \frac{n\beta H}{n\beta H + \gamma} \approx \frac{1}{2}$).
- All **$d_{\text{out}}$** directions of $\delta W_{:, i}$ ($d_{\text{out}} = 192$ for `QKV`, $64$ for `fc1`) see their curvature multiplied by **$c_i^2 \ll 1$** ($\sim 10\text{--}16\times$ flatter for `blk0_ln0` where $c_i \approx 0.25$!).
Under DevInterp's isotropic Gaussian localization $\frac{\gamma}{2}\|w - w^*\|_2^2$, each mode contributes approximately $\frac{1}{2}\frac{n\beta H_k}{n\beta H_k + \gamma}$ to $\hat\lambda$ (Gaussian/Laplace heuristic for the *estimator*). By contrast, the true LLC counts every non-degenerate direction as $\frac12$ whatever its curvature, and is unchanged by the rescaling. Pushing $d_{\text{out}} = 192$ live curvature eigenvalues below $\gamma / (n\beta)$ drives their contribution to the estimate toward $0$. That gives an apparent drop in $\hat\lambda$ from a non-orthogonal rescaling along an exact symmetry orbit alone. It also raises the local condition number by $c_i^{-4}$, which slows SGLD mixing. We did not measure Hessian spectra to confirm this account quantitatively.

## 7. Honest Nuances & Implications for Developmental Interpretability (`[empirical]` + `[conjectural]`)

1. **Both sides of the verdict are real — do not overclaim:**
   - **Developmental structure in `LR1`–`LR2` is genuinely captured by SGLD LLC (`[empirical]`):** Zero prediction (`LR1`) and in-context regression acquisition (`LR2`) drive massive, clean rises in *canonicalized* LLC (`-4.20 → 23.91 → 159.45`), with essentially zero fiber confounding (`-0.1%` in `LR1`, `-6.6%` in `LR2`).
   - **Real structural and behavioral changes *do* happen in `LR3`–`LR4`, even though the drop in the estimate does not measure them (`[empirical]`):** Final LayerNorm readout collapse (`82%` of effective unembedding weights $< 0.05$) occurs under both `Adam` and `AdamW`. OOD `g=5` degradation shows in 2 of 3 `Adam` seeds and throughout `AdamW` training. What our data do not support is reading the **`-47`-point drop in the SGLD estimate** as functional simplification. The raw and balanced points share one true LLC, and the balanced estimate is flat, so in our reproduction the drop in the estimate comes from its dependence on the `LN_0 ↔ QKV` rescaling coordinate under isotropic localization at $\gamma=13.3$ (the `AdamW` run is consistent with this). We cannot say from this whether the **true** LLC changes between 25k and 80k: both estimates are finite-$\gamma$, and we have no direct estimate of the true value. We also have not run their checkpoints or their 500k schedule.
2. **Practical prescription for DevInterp (`[paper]` + `[empirical]`):** Whenever tracking DevInterp SGLD LLC across training of LayerNorm/RMSNorm Transformers (especially with `wd = 0` or large LR schedules), practitioners should **canonicalize exact continuous symmetries (especially `LN ↔ Linear` scaling) before estimating LLC**, or report $\hat\lambda$ at a gauge-fixed representative alongside the raw estimate. Otherwise changes in a symmetry-invariant quantity cannot be told apart from drift of the estimator along a symmetry orbit. An alternative we have not tested (`[conjectural]`) is a reparametrization-aware sampler, e.g. the matched preconditioner of Lau et al. (2308.12108, App. O) or the RMSProp-preconditioned SGLD benchmarked by Hitchcock & Hoogland (2507.21449).
3. **Open question (`[conjectural]`):** Hoogland et al.'s 2-layer language model (`georgeyw/L2W256-3m-rr`, trained with `AdamW, wd = 0.05`) also exhibits a mid-training LLC drop (`LM3`); testing our exact full-model canonicalizer directly on those public Hugging Face checkpoints is an immediate natural next step.
